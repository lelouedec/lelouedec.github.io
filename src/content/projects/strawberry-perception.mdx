## The problem

Soft fruit like strawberries is delicate, grows in clusters, and is picked by hand in labour-intensive work. A harvesting robot has to answer several questions about every berry before it can touch it:

1. **Where is it?** Fruit hides behind leaves and overlaps with other fruit.
2. **What shape is it?** Size and shape decide whether a berry is worth picking and how to grip it.
3. **Which way does it point?** The gripper must approach from the right direction without bruising the fruit.

Standard 2D images struggle with all three. 3D sensing gives direct access to shape, position and occlusion, but it had mostly been used indoors or in controlled settings. This project asked how far 3D perception can go in real strawberry farms, and built methods step by step from detection to orientation.

## Step 1: Which 3D sensor? (2020)

Before building any model, we needed to know whether affordable 3D cameras can see small, soft objects in a real farm. We compared **stereo** and **time-of-flight** cameras for perceiving strawberry shape and for segmenting fruit from the background based on shape. We also compared 2D image-based and full 3D approaches on data collected at real strawberry farms.

## Step 2: Segmenting strawberries in 3D (2020–2021)

Standard deep networks for point clouds treat them as unordered sets of points. Camera-based 3D sensors do better than that, because their data is organised: every point has a pixel neighbour. We designed a **3D deep neural network that exploits this organised structure** to segment strawberries directly from the sensor output.

A simulated study also asked how good the 3D shape has to be for analysis to work, which gives sensor designers a target. An early part of this work looked at describing strawberry shape compactly with spherical harmonics.

## Step 3: Locating and counting surface features (2021)

Shape quality tells you ripeness and variety, and the small features on a berry's surface are a useful signal for it. Counting them on a 3D surface is hard because density-based methods fall apart when the features are close together or overlap.

We replaced density estimation with a **Gaussian map representation**, in which the network predicts a smooth map whose peaks mark each feature. Trained on several hundred 3D scans of strawberries from a public dataset, it was more accurate and more precise than the state-of-the-art density-based methods.

## Step 4: Orientation for picking (2023)

A robot can't pick a berry it can't orient. We introduced a **key-point-based method that predicts a strawberry's 3D orientation directly from a 2D image**. Instead of requiring a 3D reconstruction of each fruit, it finds anatomical key points and derives the orientation from them, so a standard camera is enough to set the gripper's approach.

Moving from comparing sensors, to 3D segmentation, to fine-grained features, to orientation from plain 2D images, each paper turned a limitation of the previous one into the next question. Together they form a perception stack for picking strawberries in real farm conditions.
